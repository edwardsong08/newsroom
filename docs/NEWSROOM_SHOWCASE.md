# Newsroom Showcase Brief

Version: 0.1

The blog can become a portfolio piece about deliberate human–AI collaboration:
an editorial system that is visible enough to be understood, but careful enough
not to expose a private life archive.

## Public story

Frame the project as **The Working Draft newsroom**: Edward is the publisher;
specialized agents help him remember, question, write, edit, and ship. The
interesting artifact is not a claim of autonomous intelligence. It is the
combination of clear roles, selective context, provenance, auditability, and a
distinctive newspaper presentation.

## Safe public surfaces

Future public routes or sections may include:

- **Newsroom:** a visual org chart showing the hierarchy and handoff arrows.
- **Method:** plain-language explanations of packets, privacy tiers, and audit
  events, linking to sanitized examples.
- **Article production card:** desk, genre, author/AI credit, revision, review
  gates, and source-count categories (never private source text).
- **Archive:** the normal article archive governed by
  `docs/EDITORIAL_SYSTEM.md`.
- **Demo packet:** synthetic or already-public records that visitors can inspect
  end to end.

Do not show real ChatGPT/Codex exports, Gmail messages, private Drive paths,
third-party names, credentials, or screenshots containing personal data.

## One public visual identity

Keep earlier Muggle/non-Muggle and experimental designs in Git history for
reference, but the eventual public site should present one coherent edition.
The edition can retain the newspaper character, archival texture, playful
motion, and future room/scene experiments without exposing a mode toggle or
branding that implies official Harry Potter affiliation.

## Demonstration sequence

1. Visitor submits a fictional article request.
2. Managing Editor card shows the assignment and a narrow source scope.
3. Knowledge Office card shows a timeline, index, and privacy label.
4. Editorial Board cards show premise, structure, counterpoint, and copy passes.
5. A disclosed original AI columnist or Edward produces the draft.
6. Publication Desk shows metadata placement, accessibility checks, and an audit
   event.
7. Visitor opens the final article and a sanitized “how it was made” panel.

The demo should use invented people and events or records already intended for
public release. Every sample output is labeled `DEMO` so it cannot be mistaken
for Edward's private history.

## Future interactive rooms

Classroom, law-office, and writing-desk scenes can eventually visualize cleared
`object` notes from the vault. Each object needs a provenance/permission record,
an accessible description, and a text-only article path. Motion must respect
`prefers-reduced-motion`; exploration is an optional layer, not the only way to
read the story.

## Success measures

- A new LLM can resume an assignment from the packet, working state, and audit
  log without chat history.
- A visitor can understand who did what and what context was allowed.
- No public demo reveals private or third-party information.
- An article's authorship, provenance, revision, and placement are legible.
- The visual newspaper remains quirky and memorable without making the content
  unreadable or the process unverifiable.
