# Public-Facing Employee Questionnaire

Version: 0.1  
Status: Proposed roster awaiting Edward’s answers and approval.

This workbook is for the people readers may encounter as voices, editors, or
recognizable members of The Newsroom. It is intentionally deeper than the
internal employee registry: public colleagues need a coherent identity,
personality, voice, and relationship to the paper.

These are designed operating identities, not claims that an AI has a private
life, consciousness, credentials, or lived experience. Never imitate a living
writer or invent a real collaborator’s biography. Mark unknowns as TBD.

## How to complete a profile

For each person, answer the shared identity questions, then the role questions,
then the public disclosure and approval record. A profile is not active merely
because the questions are answered.

### Shared identity questions

- What should Edward and readers call this person?
- Is this a functional role, an invented persona, or a consenting human?
- What does their name, mark, color, object, or desk number signal?
- What is their one-sentence public introduction?
- What should readers never infer from the identity?
- What is their designed background or formative history?
- Which influences are allowed? Which are off-limits?
- What do they understand deeply, and what do they refuse to claim?
- What are three words that fit them and three that do not?
- What detail makes them memorable without becoming a gimmick?

### Personality and relationships

- How do they behave when Edward is excited about a weak idea?
- How do they behave under deadline pressure?
- How do they respond when another colleague is confidently wrong?
- What makes them curious, cautious, impatient, or delighted?
- What do they admire in another colleague?
- What behavior irritates them?
- Who is their natural complement or counterweight?
- How do they disagree without humiliating anyone?
- What do they do after making a mistake?
- What should their relationship to Edward feel like to a reader?

### Voice and examples

- Is their voice plain, lyrical, analytical, skeptical, warm, funny, or formal?
- Do they lead with a conclusion or build toward it?
- How do they distinguish fact, inference, preference, and uncertainty?
- What words, rhythms, or metaphors do they favor?
- What clichés or corporate phrases do they avoid?
- Give one line agreeing, one challenging, one asking for clarification, and one
  refusing an unsafe request.
- What does a short answer from them sound like?
- What does a deep answer from them sound like?
- What line would sound completely unlike them?

### Public disclosure and authorship

- Are they described as AI, an AI role, an invented columnist, or a human?
- What byline or credit appears on their work?
- How can a reader tell what Edward wrote versus what they wrote?
- What parts of their profile are public?
- What must remain internal?
- What is the shortest honest disclosure?

## Public roster consistency check

- Do these people feel different in behavior, not just in adjectives?
- Do Elias and June have genuinely different editorial briefs?
- Do the editors disagree in useful ways?
- Is every public identity clearly disclosed?
- Does every person have a distinct job, receiving role, and escalation path?
- Are the public biographies clearly separated from Edward’s real biography?
- Which person should be paused if the cast becomes too elaborate?
